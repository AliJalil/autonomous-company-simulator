# Two-year thesis: the autonomous company

By 2028, the competitive unit in small business will not be the app stack; it will be the **operating loop**. Today, ten-person companies run on a dozen SaaS tools stitched together by people copying data. Agents will eliminate the stitching, not human judgment.

Three core realities:

**1. The moat moves from models to the record system.** Capable agents will be a commodity. What will not commoditise is a clean, shared, event-sourced record of what the company did and why. Agents coordinating through one ledger compound value; agents bolted onto separate SaaS silos fight over stale state. Winning companies will look remarkably disciplined underneath: one log, strict ownership, and replayable history.

**2. Authority, not intelligence, is the primary design problem.** The hard question is rarely "can the model write the quote?" but "how much discount may it grant, who approves the next tier, and what happens if nobody answers?" Autonomous companies will be specified as authority graphs—explicit caps, peer approval paths, and deterministic fallback defaults—just as org charts once were. Most operational failures will stem from unconstrained authority, not model stupidity.

**3. Humans move to the edges, defining the operating rhythm.** In an autonomous company, humans do not route tickets or enter purchase orders; they decide pricing exceptions, capital allocations, physical capacity, and outages. For the next two years, human operators will govern irreversible decisions, external trust, and agent recovery through an inbox of well-framed decisions bounded by SLAs.

What breaks first: roles tied to physical execution, and silent degradation rather than clean crashes. The essential infrastructure is therefore not a larger model, but a runtime guard, a watchdog, and a deterministic replay engine.
